# Handover: the Winter Fayre Sports Arcade

A small website of quick sports games for John's sports stall at the Winter Fayre on Saturday 14 November 2026. Visitors play on their own phones, reached by a QR code on the stall, and a TV at the stall shows the day's leaderboards. Sweets are given as prizes.

**The purpose of it all is to guide donations to the Parent Staff Association's Sports Fund Appeal** (John, 13 November 2026): to draw attention to what the PSA raises for sports equipment, that much of the larger equipment is paid for this way, and to encourage parents to give once or monthly. The games are the way in; the appeal is the point.

The repository is **jdsears/Winter-Fayre**. Work only in it. It was built from `jdsears/morleyu14`, which was read and never changed.

## What is built

- **The arcade** at `/`, the site's landing page: the story's picture at the very top, the heading under it with what the page is for ("Play." and "Give.") and, between them, the football ("Win.": the best keepy-uppies score on the leaderboard by 1:30pm wins a FIFA approved football; after 13:30, who has won it), the story of the appeal, then the games menu, a picture tile for each game with a Play button. This is where the QR code lands.
- **Seven games**, each on a page of its own and played full screen:
  1. Penalties (`/penalties`): five kicks each, always all five, then sudden death if it is level.
  2. Keepy-uppies (`/keepy-uppies`).
  3. Free kick challenge (`/free-kicks`): ten kicks.
  4. Head to head (`/head-to-head`): one against one for sixty seconds, with the phone held sideways.
  5. Rugby conversion (`/rugby`): five kicks at the posts, each further out, from a wider angle and into a stronger crosswind. A swipe up from the ball aims it; a longer swipe kicks harder; a curled swipe bends it. The wind is shown at the top of the screen, and in the snow and the flags on the posts. A harder kick is in the air for less time, so the wind moves it less. The wind rises steeply, as John asked after playing it: none on the first kick, then 3 to 5, 6 to 8, 9 to 11 and 12 to 14 metres a second, so from the fourth kick the ball must be aimed outside the posts to come back between them. The arrow at the top grows with the wind. The numbers are in `client/rugby/rules.js` (`SPOTS` and `WIND_PUSH`).
  6. Netball shot (`/netball`): thirty seconds to score as many goals as you can. A flick up the screen sends the ball up in an arc; the longer the flick, the further it carries. After every goal the next shot is from further back and from a different place round the goal circle; a miss is taken again from the same place, with a word on how to do better. John left the choice between this and five set shots to the build: the thirty seconds was chosen because it gives the leaderboard a spread of scores and plays faster. The shooter's own arms come up from the foot of the screen with a hand either side of the ball, and follow through as it is let go (`client/netball/arms.js`). When a goal goes in the ring turns gold, the net stretches and the screen says Goal. John asked for both after playing it.
  7. Cricket smash (`/cricket`): an over of six balls. The bowler runs in and bowls; one tap swings the bat. A swing at just the moment the ball reaches the bat is a six, a little out is a four, further out a two, a one or no run, and a swing far too early or too late misses. A miss at a ball on the stumps is out: the stumps go flying and the next batter comes in. A miss at a wide one is a dot ball. Everyone gets all six balls, so no one's go ends after one mistake: the brief said six balls and a score in runs, and that reading is the kinder one for a queue of children. The balls get quicker through the over, more of them are on the stumps, and the fourth or fifth may be a slower one. After each ball a word says how the swing was timed ("A little early", "Too late"), so a player learns as they go.
- **A leaderboard for the day for every game**: three letters and a score, the top ten shown and the top fifty one tap away. Penalties counts goals and saves; keepy-uppies counts touches; free kicks counts goals; head to head counts wins in a row; rugby counts the metres its successful kicks were taken from (130 for five from five), so the long kicks are worth the most; netball counts goals in the thirty seconds; cricket counts runs from the six balls.
- **The stall's TV screen** at `/tv`, made for a 1920 by 1080 television: the PSA's crest, the words "Scan to play" and a large QR code (505 pixels square on that screen), with the site's address under it to type, and beside it the day's leaderboards. Each game's leaderboard has the screen for nine seconds in turn, and they are asked for again every fifteen seconds. If the wifi drops, what is on the screen stays. If the site is deployed again, the screen reloads itself. It needs no touch or keyboard. On a phone it stacks, and with no script it shows every leaderboard and asks for itself again each minute.
- **The appeal's turn on the TV.** After every three leaderboards the Sports Fund Appeal fills the screen for ten seconds, in the PSA's plum and gold: the crest, "Help us fund the next chapter of school sport", what is being raised for now, what has been funded, the charity's line, and the words "Scan to donate" over a QR code of its own (505 pixels square at 1920 by 1080) with the JustGiving address under it to read. The code leads to the site's own `/donate/tv`, which counts the scan and sends the phone on to the PSA's page on JustGiving, so the numbers page can tell a scan of the TV from a tap on the Donate button. The words are the PSA's own, from `shared/appeal.js`. With no script the appeal is a band under the leaderboards, and on a phone it is a card with the Donate button in place of the code.
- **The appeal's story in 3D on the TV.** Where the television's browser can draw 3D, the appeal's turn has the story's field behind its words: the winter's night from the arcade page, filling the screen. The camera opens over the things already funded (the table tennis tables, the cricket square under its sprinkler, the rugby posts in their protectors) and drifts along them while the list "Already funded" is shown; then it comes down to the box goals, with a ball struck into the net, and draws back to show the goals and the cricket covers side by side in their rings of gold, while the list "Now raising for" is shown. So one list is on the screen at a time, larger than before, and the picture shows clear between the headline and the list. "Scan to donate", the code (505 pixels square at 1920 by 1080, as before), the address under it, the crest and the charity's line stay where they were, on the right and the left. The picture is drawn only during the appeal's ten seconds, so the television is at rest for the rest of the time, and it is drawn 1920 pixels wide at most, whatever the screen. **The appeal's turn is the screen of words it always was** where 3D will not run, where less motion has been asked for, with no script, on a screen that is not a television's shape, and on a television that draws the picture too slowly: under twenty frames a second it is first drawn with fewer pixels, and if that is not enough (under about twelve) the 3D is put away until the page is next opened. **If the picture ever looks wrong on the day, open `/tv?plain` in place of `/tv`**: that address always shows the screen of words. The code is in `client/story/screen.js`, with the camera's shot in `client/story/path.js` (`TV_SHOT`).
- **The QR code alone** at `/qr.svg`, to print for the stall. It is drawn by the server from the site's own address, so it is right whatever address the site is on.
- **Sweets, one each.** When a go reaches its game's number, the finish panel says "You have won a sweet. Show this screen at the sports stall to claim a sweet." with the time it was won and a **Stall: sweet given** button. Until that button has been tapped (twice, so a slip does not lose a child their sweet) the phone shows the waiting sweet on every finish panel and at the top of the games menu. After it, the phone says "You have had your sweet" for the rest of the day, whatever is scored. A go that falls short is told what to aim for. The arcade's tiles and the TV say what wins a sweet in each game.
- **The football.** The keepy-uppies tile, its finish panel and the TV say "Best score on the leaderboard by 1:30pm wins a FIFA approved football", with who leads. A score must be added to the leaderboard to count. At 13:30 the leader is the winner and the words change to "The football is won: ABC with 57. Come to the sports stall." The phone that set the leading score says so, and once it has won shows its winner's code. **On the day: open `/numbers` at 13:30. The football's section names the winning letters, score and code. Give the football to the phone showing the same code.** Taking an entry off the leaderboard changes who leads, before or after the close.
- **The PSA's crest**, the one picture on the site: beside "Wymondham College PSA" at the top of the arcade page, at the top of the stall's TV screen, and on the big sign across the stand in every game, to the left of its words. It is a file of about eighteen kilobytes served by the site itself (`server/crest/psa.webp`, made from the picture John sent: the whole of it, 512 pixels square). If the PSA has a sharper original, replacing that one file with it (a square WebP) is all it takes.
- **The Sports Fund Appeal** on the arcade page, between the heading and the games menu, in the PSA's plum and gold: why it matters, what has been funded (£8,500 for table tennis tables, £5,000 towards the cricket irrigation, £5,000 towards the rugby post protectors), what is being raised for now (football box goals and cricket pitch covers), and a **Donate on JustGiving** button with Gift Aid and the charity's number. The words and figures are the PSA's own and sit in one file, `shared/appeal.js`. The button goes to the site's own `/donate`, which counts the tap and sends the visitor on to JustGiving in a new tab. No money is taken on the site, and nothing about the visitor is passed on.
- **The appeal's story in 3D.** The page opens on the story's picture: a room at the top of the page, no more than half the screen, drawn as a winter's night with the five things in gold. Where a phone can draw 3D, that room is filled with the field in 3D and pinned to the top of the screen while the page is scrolled through beneath, and a camera flies over a snowy field on a winter's night, dressed as the fayre's poster is, from thing to thing as each is read about: four table tennis tables with a rally on the nearest, the cricket square under a turning sprinkler, the rugby posts in their protectors with a ball kicked over the bar, a full-sized box goal and a nine-a-side one with a ball struck into the net, and the cricket covers rolled over the strip. The two things being raised for stand in a ring of gold with sparks rising, and the last view, at "Your part", shows them side by side. The page opens low down beside the tables, looking along the row, and at the button on to the games the camera lifts to take in the whole field. The stage names what is in view in the PSA's own words ("Already funded, £8,500, Table tennis tables"). The words themselves are not changed, moved or hidden, and the Donate button is never covered. The room keeps its drawn night, and the story reads as its words, where 3D will not run, where a visitor has asked for less motion, with no script, on a screen under 480 pixels high such as a phone on its side, and for a visitor sent straight back to the games menu from a game. Because the room is in the page from the start, nothing moves when the 3D arrives. On a phone that draws the picture slowly, under twenty frames a second, it is drawn with fewer pixels from then on, so it keeps moving. The code is in `client/story/`: `path.js` (the stops and the camera), `scene.js` (the field and the things, drawn from plain shapes) and `index.js` (the scrolling).
- **The privacy page** at `/privacy`.
- **The teaser film** (not part of the site, and not in the repository, which holds no film): thirty seconds, 1080 by 1920, sent to John on 13 November 2026 with a silent copy. The cut he has is the second: it shows no code to scan, no link and no address, because it is shared the day before and the games are not to be played early, and it ends on the day, the time and the Sports Hall, with "Come and find our TV tomorrow to play!" under them. It was made from the site's own screens: each game played by its keys and filmed a frame at a time with the page's clock held, so the film is smooth on a machine that draws 3D slowly; with the landing page, the games menu (what wins a sweet, and the football prize held on the keepy-uppies tile for four seconds so it can be read) and the story being scrolled; cut together on the bars of an original jingle (sleigh bells and a glockenspiel tune, made from sine waves, no recording) with captions in Barlow Condensed. The scripts were written for the session and are not kept; a future film can be made the same way with Playwright, Pillow and ffmpeg.
- **The numbers** at `/numbers`, behind `NUMBERS_PASSWORD`: anonymous daily totals of page views, games started and finished, taps on Donate and scans of the TV's code to donate, and today's leaderboards with a **Take off** button beside every entry. If three letters appear that should not be on show, John opens `/numbers` on his phone, taps Take off, and the entry goes from the game and from the TV within a quarter of a minute.

## How the day is kept

The leaderboards and the numbers are kept in the service's memory. If a Railway volume is attached they are also written to small files on it, so a restart or a new deploy does not wipe the day. Without a volume everything still works, but a restart starts the leaderboards again. A new leaderboard starts each day (UK time) and the day before is deleted, so nothing is left after the fayre.

## Deployment

- **Railway**, from this repository. `railway.toml` installs with `npm ci`, builds with `npm run build`, starts with `npm start` and checks `/health`.
- **Variables:** `NODE_ENV=production`, `NUMBERS_PASSWORD` (John chose it and set it himself) and `SITE_ORIGIN` (the site's full address, which the QR codes carry). If `SITE_ORIGIN` is left out, the address Railway gave the service is used.
- **The site's own address** is `winterfayresports.co.uk` (13 November 2026), with and without `www`. John bought it through Cloudflare and pointed it at Railway. Cloudflare stands in front of the site: it sends the scripts compressed and keeps them near the visitor.
- **A volume is attached**, so the leaderboards and the numbers survive a restart. `/health` answers `"kept":"file"` when it is in use.
- **Every merge to `main` is deployed.** With a volume, the old copy stops before the new one starts, so each deploy takes the site away for up to a minute. Railway shows a page that says "Not Found: the train has not arrived at the station" in that minute. It comes back by itself.

## On the day

1. **The television.** Open `winterfayresports.co.uk/tv` in its browser, full screen, and leave it. It needs no touch or keyboard. The leaderboards take turns and refresh themselves, and after every three the appeal fills the screen for ten seconds with its code to scan to donate. Where the television can draw it, the appeal's turn has the 3D field moving behind its words. If that picture ever stutters or looks wrong, open `winterfayresports.co.uk/tv?plain` in its place: the same screen with the appeal as words alone.
2. **The iPad.** Keep `winterfayresports.co.uk/numbers` open, with the password. It shows page views, games played, taps on Donate and scans of the TV's code, and every entry on today's leaderboards with a **Take off** button beside it.
3. **Sweets.** A winner shows their screen at the stall. Hand over the sweet, then tap **Stall: sweet given** on their phone, and tap again to confirm. That phone then says the sweet has been had. One sweet each.
4. **The football, at 13:30.** The numbers page names the winner, by three letters and a score, with a four digit code. The winner's phone says "You have won the football" and shows the same code. Give the football to the phone that shows it.
5. **A paper QR code.** Print `winterfayresports.co.uk/qr.svg` for the stall table, once the address has been tried on a phone.
6. **Leave `main` alone during the fayre.** A merge deploys, and a deploy takes the site away for up to a minute.

## What is left

Everything in the brief, and everything John has asked for since, is built and live. Nothing is open.

Things that can be changed quickly if the day shows a need, each in one place:

- **A prize is too hard or too easy to win.** Each is one number in `shared/prizes.js`.
- **The football's time or game.** Both are in `shared/prizes.js` (`FOOTBALL`): the game's key and the cut-off as `13:30`.
- **Cricket is too hard or too easy to time.** How near the right moment a swing must be for each result is one line, `TIMING`, in `client/cricket/rules.js` (in seconds: a six is within 0.055 either side).
- **Rugby's wind.** The wind for each of the five kicks is in `SPOTS`, and how hard it pushes the ball is `WIND_PUSH`, both in `client/rugby/rules.js`.
- **How often the appeal has the TV.** `APPEAL_EVERY` (leaderboards between its turns) and `APPEAL_MS` (how long a turn lasts) in `client/tv.js`.
- **The 3D on the TV.** Needs no change to the site to turn off: open `/tv?plain`. Where the camera goes in the appeal's ten seconds is `TV_SHOT` in `client/story/path.js`, and when the list turns from what has been funded to what is being raised for is `TV_TURNS_AT` beside it.

## Working rules

See `CLAUDE.md`. In short: phones first; three letters and a score and nothing else; no trackers, no cookies, nothing from other sites; British English with no dashes; `npm test` and `npm run check` before every push; a pull request for each piece of work.
